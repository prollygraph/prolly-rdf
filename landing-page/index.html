Why use it


    

      
Version control is the data model, not a bolt-on.

      

        
Every transaction commit is a commit: an immutable, content-addressed record of the whole
          graph. Branching copies a pointer, diff walks only subtrees whose hashes differ, and a
          three-way merge finds the common ancestor — properties of the structure, not promises.
          Time-travel is the same read path pointed at an older root: ?commit=,
          ?branch=, or an RFC 7089 Accept-Datetime header.

Scans that win, measured — with the vintage on every number.

      

        
On a 500k-triple real-ontology sample at the optimized default (2026-06-13 re-measure,
          small single-box hardware — read the ratios, not the absolutes): predicate scan
          ~54× and full scan ~13× faster than RDF4J's NativeStore. On a real 103k-edge
          power-law graph, the built-in worst-case-optimal triejoin answers the cyclic triangle in
          3.2 s vs 10.4 s for RDF4J's NativeStore and 36–46 s for the
          bind-join Sails — all finishing engines agreeing on the same 131,925 triangles.

Honest limits


    

      
Bulk load is the known wall.

      

        
The per-commit tree rebuild means a 10.8M-triple ontology would not single-pass-load
          in 10 minutes where flat stores took ~3 (2026-05-31 run); chunked, bounded commits are
          the working pattern, at ~1.85× a native store's wall-clock on the 100k-batch comparison.
          If your workload is
          "load millions once, never version," a flat store loads it in minutes and this engine is
          the wrong pick.

        
the ingest story, unflinching

      

    


    

      
History costs disk; probes trail.

      

        
Content-addressed versioning stored ~3–4× a plain store's bytes on the 100k-triple
          comparison (2026-05-31; index count explains most of it, the content-addressed framing
          the rest) — that is the price of every commit staying readable. Point lookups run ~7.5×
          behind NativeStore and acyclic joins ~1.9× at the optimized default (2026-06-13) — scans
          are the strength, probes are not.

        
which shapes favor which engine

      

    


    

      
Pre-1.0 means it: single-writer, format churn.

      

        
One write transaction at a time (readers never wait — that part is by design and
          code-verified); no concurrent-writer rebase yet. The on-disk format evolves without
          backwards compatibility until 1.0 — export/re-import is the supported transition.

How it works, in three sentences

    
A prolly
      tree is a B-tree whose node boundaries are chosen by a rolling hash over content, so the
      same data always builds the same tree and every node is stored under the hash of its own
      bytes. A commit is one small record naming the roots of the four quad indexes plus the term
      dictionary — old roots stay valid forever, which is why
      a new version shares almost
      everything with the last and a historical read costs about what a HEAD read costs.
      The Sail puts standard SPARQL on top, so
      RDF4J tooling works unchanged.
